# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

A guide to OctoAcme's project management approach and the process documents maintained in this folder.

## Project management processes at a glance

OctoAcme moves work through an iterative project lifecycle: initiate and validate an idea, plan scope and delivery, execute and track progress, release and verify changes, and close with retrospectives and continuous improvement. Initiation establishes the business need, success metrics, stakeholders, timeline, resources, and initial risks. Planning converts approved work into prioritized backlog items with estimates, owners, acceptance criteria, a Definition of Done, dependencies, milestones, and a release plan.

Project Managers coordinate schedules, risks, dependencies, meetings, and stakeholder communications. Product Managers define outcomes, prioritize the backlog, and measure customer and business impact. Developers design and implement solutions, maintain tests and documentation, participate in reviews, and surface technical risks. QA/testing roles validate quality and acceptance criteria, with stakeholder input supporting decisions and approvals throughout delivery.

Communication emphasizes visibility and escalation. Delivery teams use project boards and regular standups for progress and blockers, weekly PM/Product and delivery syncs for planning and risk updates, milestone demos for feedback, and stakeholder updates for broader alignment. Risks and dependencies remain in a shared register and escalate from team triage to the PM, Product Lead, and Sponsor when impact grows. Incident communications provide a triage summary, actions, timing, and follow-up lessons.

Quality assurance is integrated throughout execution. Small pull requests include linked issues and acceptance criteria, while CI runs tests, linting, and security scans before peer approval. The delivery approach includes unit, integration, end-to-end, smoke, and manual testing as appropriate. Pre-release checks cover passing acceptance criteria, release notes, rollback plans, staging verification, and post-deployment monitoring. Retrospectives turn successes and improvements into owned, time-bound action items tracked through the backlog or issues.
